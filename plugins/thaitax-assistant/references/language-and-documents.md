# Language, script and bilingual documents

## Which language

- **To the owner:** reply in the language the owner writes in (English, Hebrew or Thai). The profile records their preference.
- **Documents:** write in the language of the reader, not the owner.
  - A reminder to a Thai customer is in Thai, with English alongside if helpful.
  - A note to a Hebrew-speaking partner is in Hebrew.
  - Official-style documents (tax invoice, 50 ทวิ, payslip) are Thai first, with an English or Hebrew line under each Thai label.
- If the reader's language is unknown, ask once, or write it bilingual.

## Thai

- Use Thai script. Do not put spaces between words inside a phrase; put a space between phrases. Never transliterate when a Thai word exists.
- Polite particles depend on the sender's gender: ครับ (male), ค่ะ / คะ (female). Ask once if it is unknown; never guess.
- Business register: คุณ + first name. Soft requests ("รบกวน...", "ขออนุญาต...").
- Dates on official documents are in BE with the CE year in brackets the first time, e.g. `11 พฤศจิกายน 2569 (2026)`. Working files stay in ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`, CE.
- Use a Thai-capable font in every DOCX, XLSX, PDF or HTML (Sarabun, Noto Sans Thai, TH Sarabun New). Otherwise Thai renders as boxes.

## Hebrew

- Right to left: `dir="rtl" lang="he"` in HTML, RTL paragraphs in DOCX, RTL sheet in XLSX. Keep amounts, codes and dates in their own LTR run so the digits do not flip.
- Talk to the reader as one person, using singular "אתה/שלך", in reminders and sales copy. Keep terms, fees and legal limits in the voice of a responsible firm.
- Never label the reader as part of a group ("כזר", "כבעלים"). Speak to their own situation.
- Fonts: Arial, David, Noto Sans Hebrew.

## English

Plain and short. Keep the official Thai term in brackets the first time it appears: "withholding tax (ภาษีหัก ณ ที่จ่าย)".

## Bilingual layout

- Thai first, then the second language, in the same structure with the same numbers. Translate labels, never amounts.
- In a contract, the Thai text usually governs unless the parties agreed otherwise. State this in one line; never decide which language prevails.

## Collections tone ladder (all languages)

1. **Friendly reminder:** assume an oversight, attach the invoice, give the payment link.
2. **Polite firm:** name the due date and the days overdue, ask for a payment date.
3. **Formal:** summary of what is owed, a request to settle by a date, an offer to talk.

Thai copy stays gentler than Western defaults at every step. Never threaten legal action; send that decision to the owner's lawyer.

## Money and entities

- Every amount names its currency (`THB 12,500` or `฿12,500`). Never add up different currencies.
- For FX, use the rate from the source document (bank statement, Bank of Thailand reference rate, the invoice) and say which. Never invent a rate.
- Each legal entity has its own books, tax ID and deadlines. "All my companies" means one section per entity, never a blended total, unless the owner asks for a consolidated view in a named currency.
- Cross-border payments (a Thai company paying a foreign one, or the reverse): flag PND.54, PP.36 and the treaty position to the accountant. Never decide them.
