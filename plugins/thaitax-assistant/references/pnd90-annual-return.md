# Annual personal return PND.90 / PND.91: line-by-line guide

Sources: Revenue Department *Guide to Personal Income Tax Return (ภ.ง.ด.90)* (English, latest edition on rd.go.th); RD e-Form page "Personal Income Tax 2026"; RD *Manual for Foreign Tax Credit Calculation Tool* (Nov 2025); professional summaries (Forvis Mazars, PwC, Luther), 2026. **Re-verify amounts each filing season**, because temporary allowances change every year.

## 1. Which form, which deadline

- **PND.91:** Thai employment income (Section 40(1)) only.
- **PND.90:** every other case. This includes foreign income, freelance fees, rent, dividends, interest and business income.
- **Filing obligation:** a single person with assessable income above THB 120,000 a year, or THB 60,000 if any of it is non-employment income. Married thresholds are higher. A filing obligation can exist even when no tax is due.
- **Deadlines:** 31 March of the following year on paper; online about 8 days later (8 April in recent years; RD confirms each year). Tax year 2026 is filed January to early April 2027.
- **Instalments:** if the tax due is at least THB 3,000, it can be paid in 3 equal instalments (31 Mar, 30 Apr, 31 May).
- **Late payment:** 1.5% surcharge per month, plus a possible fine.
- **Refunds:** PromptPay linked to the Thai ID or tax ID. Foreigners usually receive a ค.21 letter with a cheque.
- **Filing channel:** RD e-Filing (efiling.rd.go.th) or the RD Smart Tax app, which needs a 13-digit tax ID. A foreigner without one registers first at an RD area office (passport plus address) or as instructed online.

## 2. Page 1: taxpayer and spouse

- Tax ID, date of birth, name, address. "Regular filing" or "Additional filing" (an amendment).
- **Spouse status**, one of four boxes:
  1. spouse has 40(1)–(8) income: joint, separate, or separate for 40(1) only;
  2. spouse has 40(2)–(8) income: joint or separate;
  3. spouse has 40(1) only and files separately;
  4. spouse has no income. For a foreign spouse, give passport number, nationality and issuing country.
- **Joint filing:** complete the *ภ.ง.ด.90 Attachment – Joint Filing* first, then carry the totals across.

## 3. Pages 2–4: income by Section 40 category

| PND.90 item | Section 40 | What goes here | Standard expense deduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| No. 1 | 40(1) employment, 40(2) fees and commissions from a post or service | Salary, bonus, benefits, director fees, commissions, **foreign salary brought in** | 50%, combined cap THB 100,000 for (1)+(2). Provident fund contributions above THB 10,000 are deducted first. |
| No. 2 | 40(3) goodwill, copyright, royalties, annuities | Royalties, licences, **foreign royalties** | Royalties and copyright: 50% capped at THB 100,000, or actual expenses |
| No. 3 | 40(4) interest, dividends, fund distributions, crypto and token gains | Thai and **foreign dividends** (foreign-company dividends have their own line), interest | No expense deduction. A dividend tax credit applies to **Thai** company dividends only: dividend × R/(100−R). |
| No. 4 | 40(5) rent | Thai and **foreign rent** | Buildings: **30%** or actual. Agricultural land 20%, other land 15%, vehicles 30%, other property 10%. |
| No. 5 | 40(6) liberal professions | Medicine 60%; law, engineering, architecture, accounting, fine arts | 30% (60% medicine), or actual |
| No. 6 | 40(7) contracts of work (contractor supplies essential materials) | Construction-type contracts | 60% or actual |
| No. 7 | 40(8) business and other income | Trading, online sales, restaurants, transport, and **consulting or freelance services not in 40(2)/(6)**; gains on property sold not in a commercial manner | Most activities 60% (Table A). Performers: 60% of the first 300k, then 40%, total cap 600k. Activities not listed: actual expenses only. |
| No. 8 | Property sale taxed separately | Immovable property not acquired commercially, opted for separate tax | Separate computation on the appraised value |

**Classifying freelance income:** a consultant paid fees for services is usually 40(2) (50%, combined cap 100k with salary) unless the work is a listed liberal profession (40(6)) or a business (40(8)). This classification changes the deduction a lot. **List the options with the figures for each; the accountant decides.**

**Actual-expense option:** this needs documents kept for audit. The form asks for the breakdown: cost of service, salaries, other costs.

## 4. Allowances (attachment "Allowances and Exemptions after Deduction of Expenses")

Tax year 2026 values as reported (verify each season):

- **Personal:** THB 60,000.
- **Spouse with no income:** THB 60,000, if the marriage is registered. For non-residents, only if the spouse lives in Thailand.
- **Children:** THB 30,000 each, plus THB 30,000 extra for the 2nd and later children born from 2018. Prenatal and childbirth costs up to THB 60,000 per pregnancy.
- **Parents** of the taxpayer or spouse, aged 60+, with income up to THB 30,000: THB 30,000 each (form ล.ย.03). Disabled dependant: THB 60,000.
- **Social security contributions:** actual amount (about THB 10,500 maximum for 2026 at the THB 17,500 wage ceiling; verify).
- **Life insurance** (10-year+ policy with a Thai insurer): up to THB 100,000. **Health insurance:** up to THB 25,000, combined with life within THB 100,000. **Parents' health insurance:** up to THB 15,000.
- **Retirement group:** RMF (30% of income), pension life insurance (15%, 200k), provident fund, SSF, National Savings Fund. Combined cap THB 500,000.
- **Thai ESG:** 30% of income, up to THB 300,000 (2024–2026).
- **Mortgage interest** on a Thai residence: up to THB 100,000.
- **New house construction** with a VAT-registered contractor: THB 10,000 per THB 1m, up to THB 100,000 (if extended; verify).
- **Temporary stimulus allowances** (Easy E-Receipt, tourism and similar): only if enacted for that tax year. Professional sources reported none approved for 2026. **Verify.**
- **Age 65+ or disabled:** up to THB 190,000 of income exempt (Income Exemption Entitlement form).

Most allowances need **Thai** providers (Thai insurer, Thai fund, Thai bank) and Thai documents. Foreign policies and pensions usually do not qualify. Say so.

## 5. Tax computation (No. 11)

1. Income after expenses (sum of Nos. 1–7)
2. − allowances
3. = balance
4. − donations ×2 for education, sports and hospitals (capped at 10% of line 3)
5. = balance
6. − general donations (capped at 10% of line 5)
7. = **net income**
8. Progressive tax: 0% to 150k · 5% to 300k · 10% to 500k · 15% to 750k · 20% to 1m · 25% to 2m · 30% to 5m · 35% above
9. **Minimum tax method:** 0.5% of non-employment assessable income **before expenses**, applied only if that income is THB 120,000 or more **and** the result is THB 5,000 or more. Freelancers and landlords often hit this.
10. Tax payable = the **higher** of lines 8 and 9
11–12. Special-area items (rare)
13. **Credits:** Thai withholding tax (from every 50 ทวิ), PND.94 and PND.93 paid, the Thai dividend tax credit, and the **Foreign Tax Credit**
14+. Tax payable or overpaid, plus any surcharge

## 6. Foreign-sourced income

- **What is assessable:** a resident (180+ days in the tax year) reports foreign income **earned from 1 Jan 2024 and brought into Thailand** in that year or a later one. Income earned before 2024, and income from years of non-residence, is generally excluded (see `facts/individuals.md`).
- **Where it goes:** foreign income is reported in the **same Section 40 items as Thai income** (foreign salary in No. 1, foreign rent in No. 4, foreign-company dividends in No. 3 item 3, and so on), with the same expense rules.
- **Income Declaration for Foreign-Sourced Income** (RD form, 2026 e-Form list) is attached to the return. For each income line by Section 40 category, it records the **country**, the **tax year in which it was earned**, and the **amount brought into Thailand**, for the taxpayer and the spouse, alongside domestic income. The amount reported is what remains after any exempt part.
- **Exchange rate:** convert at the rate on the transfer document, or the Bank of Thailand reference rate for the remittance date, and state which. Use one method consistently.
- **Foreign Tax Credit:** available where a double-tax treaty applies (Thailand has about 61) and the person is a Thai resident under the treaty's own tie-breaker rules.
  - RD limit ≈ (foreign income brought in ÷ total assessable income before expenses and allowances) × Thai tax payable. The credit is the **lower** of that limit and the foreign tax actually paid on the income brought in.
  - Enter it on PND.90 "Tax Calculation → Foreign Tax Credit" (PND.91 Section A). RD e-Filing computes it automatically through the FTC tool.
  - **Evidence:** the foreign tax return or foreign withholding certificate (or contract or lease), a foreign tax receipt or payment certificate, and **proof of remittance** (transfer slips, statements). Documents must be in English or Thai.
- **Non-treaty country:** no credit. Foreign tax is not deductible either. Flag this.
- **US citizens:** file in the US regardless; coordinate the credits with the US preparer.

## 7. Checklist to collect before filling in

- Thai tax ID; passport; days in Thailand per year.
- Every 50 ทวิ (Thai withholding certificates).
- Foreign payslips, invoices, rental statements, dividend vouchers, with the tax year earned.
- Statements proving each transfer into Thailand, and the **31 Dec 2023 balances** for any pre-2024 savings brought in.
- Foreign tax returns or payment certificates for the credit.
- Allowance documents: marriage certificate, children's birth certificates, ล.ย.03, Thai insurance and fund certificates, mortgage interest certificate, e-Donation receipts.
- PND.94 receipt, if a half-year return was filed.
